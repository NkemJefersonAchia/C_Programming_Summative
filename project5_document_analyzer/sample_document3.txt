Modern Cameroon: 1972 to Today
In 1972, a national vote ended the federation and created the United Republic of Cameroon.
Ahmadou Ahidjo resigned in 1982, and Paul Biya became president.
In 1984, the country took the name Republic of Cameroon again.
Multiparty politics returned in the early 1990s after years of single-party rule.
Cameroonians speak French and English, which are the two official languages.
Since 2016, tensions in the English-speaking regions have affected daily life.
Football, music and a strong sense of unity remain central to the culture here.
Yaounde is the capital city, and Douala is the largest city and main port.
