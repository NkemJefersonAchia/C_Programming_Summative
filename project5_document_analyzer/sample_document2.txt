From Mandates to Independence in Cameroon
After the First World War, the League of Nations divided Cameroon between France and Britain.
France governed the larger eastern part, called French Cameroon.
Britain governed two narrow western territories, known as British Cameroons.
In the 1950s, the UPC party led by Ruben Um Nyobe demanded independence and reunification.
French Cameroon became independent on 1 January 1960, with Ahmadou Ahidjo as its first president.
In February 1961, the people of the Southern Cameroons voted to join the Republic of Cameroon.
On 1 October 1961, the two parts formed the Federal Republic of Cameroon.
The Northern Cameroons voted to join Nigeria instead.
