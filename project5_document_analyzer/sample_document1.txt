Early History and the Colonial Period of Cameroon
Long before European contact, many peoples lived in the land that is now Cameroon.
The Sao civilization flourished near Lake Chad, and Bantu-speaking groups settled the forests of the south.
In 1472, Portuguese sailors reached the Wouri River and named it the River of Prawns.
That name later became Cameroon, from the Portuguese word camaroes.
In the early 1800s, Fulani leaders built powerful states in the northern highlands.
In 1884, Germany signed treaties with the Duala chiefs and created the protectorate of Kamerun.
The Bamum kingdom in the west, led by Sultan Njoya, created its own writing system.
German rule in Cameroon ended during the First World War.
